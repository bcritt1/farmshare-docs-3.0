NSF ACCESS is a national program, funded by the U.S. National Science
Foundation, that gives researchers and educators time on supercomputers and
other computing systems at institutions around the country. It isn't run by
Stanford Research Computing.

## When ACCESS Is Useful

ACCESS is an option when your work needs more computing than FarmShare's limits
allow, or hardware FarmShare doesn't have, and it doesn't fit on
[Sherlock](sherlock.md). Allocations are free to the people who receive them.

It's also used for teaching. The smaller project types are meant for classes and
training events as well as research.

## Allocation Types

ACCESS has four project types, from small to large:

| Type | Meant for | Review |
|---|---|---|
| Explore | Trying out resources, graduate student projects, small classes | Short request, checked for eligibility |
| Discover | Research with modest needs, graduate student projects, large classes | Short request, checked for eligibility |
| Accelerate | Experienced researchers with mid-scale needs | Longer proposal with panel review |
| Maximize | Large-scale research | Full proposal with panel review, twice a year |

The [ACCESS project types](https://allocations.access-ci.org/project-types) page
gives the credit limits and proposal lengths for each.

## Who Can Apply

To lead an ACCESS project, you have to be a U.S.-based researcher or educator,
at the graduate student level or higher. Graduate students can lead Explore and
Discover projects, and their advisor has to be part of the request.
Undergraduates can't lead a project, but they can work on one led by an eligible
advisor or instructor. Instructors can lead projects for their classes.

The [ACCESS allocations
policy](https://allocations.access-ci.org/allocations-policy) has the full
rules.

## Getting Started

Create an ACCESS account with your Stanford email address, then request an
allocation. See [ACCESS for graduate
students](https://access-ci.org/get-started/for-graduate-students/) and [ACCESS
Allocations](https://allocations.access-ci.org/) for the steps.
